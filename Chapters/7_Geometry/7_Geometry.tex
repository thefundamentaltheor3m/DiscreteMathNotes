\chapter{Discrete Geometry} \label{Ch7:CH}
\thispagestyle{empty}

We now put points in the plane and ask how many pairs of them can be at distance exactly $1$. In the language of \Cref{Ch2:Def:Unit_Distance_Graph}, this asks how many edges a subgraph of the unit distance graph on $n$ vertices can have, rather than how many colours it needs. Constructions give lower bounds; the upper bound will come from drawing graphs in the plane and counting how often their edges are forced to cross. The same counting then bounds how often points can lie on lines. The rest of the chapter is about lines in $\R^3$ and over finite fields, and its tool is polynomials of low degree.
