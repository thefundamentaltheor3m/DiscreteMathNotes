\chapter{Probability} \label{Ch1:CH}
\thispagestyle{empty}

Almost every argument in these notes produces an object by showing that a random one works, so the vocabulary for talking about random objects had better come first. This chapter collects it. Very little of what follows is new---probability spaces, expectation, variance---and none of it is proved at length; what matters is that the two inequalities the rest of the course uses by name, Markov's and Chebyshev's, are written down somewhere rather than assumed. Conditional expectation gets a little more care than the rest, because \Cref{Ch4:CH} leans on it hard enough to notice.

The course itself begins in \Cref{Ch2:CH}, and a reader who would rather see a graph than a sigma-algebra can start there and come back when a probability turns up.
