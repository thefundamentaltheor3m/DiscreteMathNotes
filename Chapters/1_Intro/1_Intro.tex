\chapter{The Probabilistic Method} \label{Ch1:CH}
\thispagestyle{empty}

Much of what follows is proved by choosing at random and showing that a bad outcome is improbable, so that a good one has to exist somewhere. A random coloring bounds $m(k)$ below in \Cref{Ch2:Thm:mk_Lower_Bound}, a random permutation proves Sperner's theorem and then \Cref{Ch3:Thm:EKR}, and a random set followed by a random graph gives us \Cref{Ch2:Thm:Erdos_Girth}. This chapter collects what those arguments run on---two inequalities that bound a tail probability in terms of the expectation, and the random graph itself---and then ends on a question the expectation alone cannot settle.
