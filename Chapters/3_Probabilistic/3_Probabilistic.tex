\chapter{The Probabilistic Method} \label{Ch3:CH}
\thispagestyle{empty}

Random choices have already done a good deal of work for us. A random coloring bounded $m(k)$ below in \Cref{Ch1:Thm:mk_Lower_Bound}, a random permutation proved Sperner's theorem and then \Cref{Ch2:Thm:EKR}, and a random set and then a random graph gave us \Cref{Ch1:Thm:Erdos_Girth}. Every one of those arguments had the same shape: show that a bad outcome has probability less than one, and conclude that a good outcome exists somewhere. This chapter treats the method as a subject rather than as a tool, which means starting with the two inequalities that say how far a random variable can stray from its mean, and then asking something the mean alone cannot settle.
