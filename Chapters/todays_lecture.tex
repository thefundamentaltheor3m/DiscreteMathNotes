\begin{boxlnotation}
    Given a graph $G$ and $p \in [0, 1]$, define $G_p$ to be the graph that retains vertices of $G$ independently with probability $p$ (ie, that deletes vertices of $G$ independently with probability $1 - p$). \end{boxlemma}

\end{proof}

\begin{boxdefinition}[Joint]
    A \textbf{joint} in $\R^3$ is an intersection point of $3$ non-coplanar lines.
\end{boxdefinition}

In other words, any point at which three lines that do not share a plane in common meet is called a joint.

Let's now take a detour into the world of polynomials.

Our notation for this course isn't amazing. Let $\F$ be a field.

\begin{boxnotation}
    $\poly_D\of{\F^n}$ denotes the set
    \begin{align*}
        \setst{p \in \F\!\brac{x_1, \ldots, x_n}}{\deg_{\tot}\of{p} \leq D}
    \end{align*}
    where $\deg_{\tot}$ denotes the total degree of the polynomial.
\end{boxnotation}

The monomials form a basis of this space. By a stars and bars argument, we can show that the number of monomials is $\binom{D + n}{n}$. So this is the dimension of this space.

Observe the following: if $S \ssq \F^n$ is such that $\abs{S} \leq \dim\of{\poly_D\of{\F^n}}$, there is some $p \in \poly_D\of{\F^n}$ that vanishes on $S$. The proof is simple: consider the evaluation map
\begin{align*}
    \ev : \poly_D\of{\F^n} \to \F^{S} : p \mapsto \parenth{p(s)}_{s \in S}
\end{align*}
Rank-nullity\footnote{Tarun would be so proud!} and the inequality $\abs{S} \leq \parenth{\text{dimension of space}}$ tells us the dimension of the kernel is positive, which gives us some $p$ as desired.
